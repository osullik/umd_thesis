\section{Graph Classes}
\label{section:22-graph-classes}

Graphs can be constructed synthetically, or they can come from real data.
The size and topology directly impact the effectiveness and efficiency of graph processing. 
While the structure of graphs exists on a spectrum and is influenced by several factors, it is useful to identify key categories and their characteristics. 
\textit{Random, Scale-Free} and \textit{Mesh} are commonly identified groupings, with \textit{non-native} graphs emerging as another category.

\subsection{Random Graphs}
A random graph is synthetic. 
It is generated by creating a set of vertices and then creating an edge between each vertex in the set with a given probability. 
To improve the `realism' of the graph, the probabilities can be derived from some pre-existing distribution.
Despite its limited applicability in the real world, a \textit{Uniform Random Graph} is the typical baseline for evaluating graph algorithms. 
One of the most well-known random graph generation models is the Erd\H{o}s-R\'enyi model~\cite{Erdhos1960}.

\subsection{Scale-Free Graphs}

\define{Scale-Free Graph}{Graphs where the vertex degrees follow a power-law distribution. Common in social networks, they form because the number of vertices tends to increase over time, and new vertices preferentially attach to vertices that already have high degrees~\cite{Barabasi1999a}. They tend to have a small diameter (the `small world' property) a large variance in neighborhood size, and a `global-neighborhood' characteristic.}\\

\textit{Graph-Native Problems} are problems that have an intrinsic graph structure. For example, a social media friends graph.
\textit{Non-Native Graph Problems} are problems that have been transformed into graph problems for processing. 
For example, aggregating pixels by color to vertices and drawing edges between the vertices to represent an image in lower dimensional space~\cite{Cen2023}.
Non-native graph formats are introducing new graph shapes that will likely have further unpredictable downstream impacts on the effectiveness and efficiency of graph processing.
