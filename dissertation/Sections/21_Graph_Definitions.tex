\section{Graph Definitions}
\label{section:21-graph-definitions}

This section will introduce key notation and definitions used throughout the thesis. 

The following definitions describe the commonly agreed building blocks and descriptors of a graph in an abstract sense. 
\define{Vertex ($V$)}{Represent the `entities' or `things'. Also called a node.}
\define{Edge ($E$)}{Represent the relationships between vertices. Also called arcs or links.}
\define{Graph ($G$)}{The data structures comprising of the related vertices and edges such that $G=\{V,E\}$. Also called a `network'.}
\define{Topology}{The structure of a graph is created when edges link vertices together. The defining feature of graphs.}
\define{Attribute}{The labels, properties or other information that is encoded in the vertices or edges of a graph. Optional in graphs.}
\define{Weight}{The numerical value assigned to an edge to describe its importance in a graph. Optional in graphs.}
\define{Direction}{The flow of information across an edge. If a graph is directed, the order of source and destination vertex is important. If undirected, vertex order is not important.}

The following definitions describe the aggregations, generalizations and descriptors of graphs: 
\define{Degree}{The count of edges at a vertex. Frequently split between degree$_{out}$ and degree$_{in}$ in directed graphs. Larger average degrees indicate denser graphs.}
\define{Diameter}{The shortest path between the two most distant vertices in a graph. Sparser graphs tend to have larger diameters.}
\define{Heterogeneity}{Describes a graph with multiple `types' of vertex, for example, a social graph that has vertices to represent `person' and vertices to represent `organization'.}
\define{Static}{A graph that is known apriori and does not change.}
\define{Dynamic}{A graph that evolves with edges being created, updated and deleted over time. Also called a streaming graph.}



The following definitions describe the characteristics of graphs: 
\define{Degree Distribution}{The distribution of vertex degrees in a given graph. Can follow any statistical distribution.}
