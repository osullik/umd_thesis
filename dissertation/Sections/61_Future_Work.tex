\section{Future Work}
\label{section:61-Future-Work}

The \gai benchmark framework is in its infancy. 
It has many avenues for future work, including simple implementation and engineering challenges, the development of a complementary maturity model, support for dynamic inputs, cost measurement, and further development of constraint-based experimentation.


\textbf{Engineering challenges.}
Many engineering and data collection problems must be addressed to build the benchmark system, including expanding the dataset coverage using synthetic data generators, adding a linear algebraic approach to \ac{CD}, and testing the benchmark on a distributed \ac{SUT}, a shared-memory parallel \ac{SUT} and hardware-accelerated \ac{SUT}. 

\textbf{A Benchmarking Maturity Model.}
After reviewing many benchmark approaches, we have identified that there appears to be an implicit maturity model in benchmark development. 
For a given field, early benchmarks focus on proving functionality.
Over time, the focus shifts to improving the effectiveness relative to other solutions and, finally, to making solutions more efficient. 
Poorly constructed maturity models resulting from a related study and compiled without a formal methodology have gained negative attention recently~\cite{Lahrmann2011, Sadiq2021}. 
So, the formal development of a maturity model for benchmarking was scoped out of this thesis.
However, it will be helpful for future work in investigating the correlation between benchmark availability and field maturity.

\textbf{Dynamic Graph Processing.}
The current \gai benchmark can evaluate static and dynamic problems together but does not have the datasets or the transfer mechanism to feed datasets dynamically to the \ac{SUT}.
% An important avenue of future work to emphasize the importance of investing in dynamic graph processing is extending the \gai benchmark framework observation apparatus to support dynamic graphs. 
An important avenue of future work is to extend the \gai benchmark framework observation apparatus to support dynamic graphs, which we will implement with a `push' based system configured in the same fashion as the current polling system.

\textbf{Automated Hypothesis Evaluation.}
Our design process involves the development of hypotheses to guide the design of the investigation apparatus and collection of data. 
Creating an alert mechanism to notify a benchmark user when a hypothesis is violated is a valuable augmentation to the current approach. 
It will boost the utility of the benchmark and move it further away from being a tool for `racing systems' to a platform for meaningful research.

\textbf{Non-Invasive Cost Measurement.}
The major limitation of the \gai benchmark framework is its inability to measure cost accurately.
Prior approaches focus on estimation using a cost model or selecting a proxy metric like power consumption as the basis of cost estimates. 
We must develop a method to measure cost from the Input/Output interface to add the third crucial performance metric that should be considered alongside efficiency and effectiveness.
Alternatively, developing a method to characterize a remote system so that the benchmark framework can estimate costs for the system under test may be feasible by providing a micro benchmarking task that a \ac{SUT} runs to provide the seed value for a predictive cost model.
However, a micro benchmarking approach will break down when using bespoke hardware accelerators that may not support the benchmark as written or whose engineering breaks the assumptions of a cost model. 

\textbf{Constraint-Based Experimentation.}
Real-world \gai systems must operate under efficiency, effectiveness, and cost constraints. 
The experiments in Section~\ref{section:56-CD-Results} show how efficiency and effectiveness can be easily tracked and limited to simulate constrained environments. 
The \gai benchmark system can be easily extended for exploratory experimentation to investigate how \gai will perform under specific constraints beyond what has been explored by the benchmark in terms of evaluating hardware, representation, and implementation variations.
Extending the experiments will evolve the \gai benchmark framework into a platform for simulating more complicated `what-if' scenarios, potentially simulating edge-computing, low \ac{SWaP} thresholds, and other business or policy constraints.
