\section{Future Work}
\label{section:61-Future-Work}

The \gai benchmark framework is in its infancy and has many avenues for future work. 
Many engineering and data collection problems must be addressed to build the benchmark system, including rectifying the deficiencies in the dataset coverage, choice of metric, and breadth of implementations the Population Segmentation experiments found.
Specific engineering challenges of immediate interest are adding a linear algebraic approach to community detection and testing the benchmark on distributed shared-memory parallel and hardware-accelerated systems. 

\textbf{A Benchmarking Maturity Model.}
Reviewing many benchmark approaches suggests an implicit maturity model in benchmark development. 
For a given field, early benchmarks focus on proving functionality.
Over time, the focus shifts to improving the effectiveness of others' solutions and, finally, to making solutions more efficient. 
Poorly constructed maturity models have gained negative attention recently~\cite{Lahrmann2011, Sadiq2021}. 
So, the formal development of a maturity model for benchmarking was scoped out of this thesis. 
However, it will be helpful for future work in investigating the correlation between benchmark availability and field maturity. 

\textbf{Monotonically Improving Algorithms.}
The Population Segmentation benchmark problems reveal that trading off effectiveness and efficiency requires monotonically improving solutions for incremental and approximate algorithms. 
Further work must identify existing algorithms with these guarantees across the \gai framework. 
For problems where none exist, the real-world drivers identified in this thesis provide a solid incentive to develop them.

\textbf{Dynamic Graph Processing.}
The current \gai benchmark can evaluate static and dynamic problems together but does not have the datasets or the transfer mechanism to feed datasets dynamically to the \ac{SUT}.
An important avenue of future work to emphasize the importance of investing in dynamic graph processing is extending the \gai benchmark framework observation apparatus to support dynamic graphs. 

\textbf{Automated Hypothesis Evaluation.}
Our design process involves the development of hypotheses to guide the design of the investigation apparatus and collection of data. 
Encoding the hypotheses so that they alert when a benchmark submission violates their assumptions is a valuable augmentation to the current approach.
Violating the `conventional wisdom' they represent likely indicates an innovative approach or manipulating the system at worst. 
Improving the automated evaluation will boost the utility of the benchmark and move it away from being a tool for `racing systems' to a platform for meaningful research.

\textbf{Non-Invasive Cost Measurement.}
The major limitation of the \gai benchmark framework is its inability to measure a cost accurately.
Current approaches focus on estimation using a cost model or selecting a proxy metric like power consumption as the basis of cost estimates. 
We must develop a method to measure cost from the Input/Output interface to add the third crucial dimension to the efficiency and effectiveness metrics already collected. 
Alternatively, developing a method to characterize a remote system so that the benchmark framework can estimate costs for the system under test may be feasible. 
However, it is limited by using bespoke hardware accelerators that may not support a general-purpose tool running on their system to collect that information.

\textbf{Constraint-Based Experimentation.}
The real-world requirements of \gai require the systems to operate under efficiency, effectiveness, and cost constraints. 
The experiments in section~\ref{section:56-CD-Results} show how efficiency and effectiveness can be easily tracked and limited to simulate constrained environments. 
The \gai benchmark system can be easily extended for exploratory experimentation to investigate how \gai will perform under constraints, making it highly suited for simulating edge-computing, low \ac{SWaP} thresholds, and other business or policy constraints.
