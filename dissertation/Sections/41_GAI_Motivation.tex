\section{Motivation}
\label{section:41-GAI-Motivation}

In reviewing prior work in competitive benchmarking and workload analysis (section~\ref{section:31-literature-benchmarking}), we conclude that purely competitive `black-box' benchmarks from the Input/Output paradigm lack descriptive power and that `white-box' empirical benchmarks from the workload analyses paradigm are too invasive for general-purpose use. 
The review of \gai benchmarking (section~\ref{section:32-literature-benchmarking-gai}) also concludes that existing \gai task, dataset, and metric coverage is narrow, neglects dynamic processing of heterogeneous graphs and fails to evaluate approximate and incremental solutions appropriately. 
Our `grey-box' approach addresses these issues with an Input/Output model that is agnostic to the configuration of the \ac{SUT}, fuses the empirical study of algorithms with constraint theory to design problem-centric tasks and broad coverage of datasets that stimulate known system bottlenecks, and uses a nuanced view of efficiency and effectiveness to compare static and dynamic approaches in the same framework.

Problem-centric tasks and broad dataset coverage are essential components of the \gai benchmark framework design. 
We follow a problem-centric design to specify tasks by the inputs and outputs rather than the typical system-centric abstract kernel approach that specifies which process should be applied to generate a solution.
The problem-centric focus mimics real-world problems and builds in the \ac{ETL} and multi-step problems beyond prior work's graph kernels. 
The \gai framework offers a powerful tool to estimate the system's internal state without needing internal access by combining the problem-centric focus with datasets designed to stimulate known system bottlenecks and algorithmic weaknesses.
Furthermore, by monitoring the effectiveness of results produced by the \ac{SUT} over time and designing problems to have answers with degrees of correctness, our benchmark framework can compare deterministic, non-deterministic, and approximate approaches of both static and dynamic graph processing methodologies in a single framework.
The breadth of coverage allows a benchmark user to experiment with many different approaches, explore trading effectiveness for efficiency, and experiment with working under efficiency, effectiveness, and cost constraints.
