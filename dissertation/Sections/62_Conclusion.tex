%Restate Thesis Statement
This thesis has developed a `grey-box' \gai benchmarking framework that fuses the empirical evaluation of algorithms with constraint theory to answer \textit{if, how} and \textit{when} to speed up \gai processing given real-world efficiency, effectiveness, and cost constraints. 
By exploring the landscape of \gai and identifying key challenges, we have formulated a novel approach to benchmarking that moves beyond traditional `black-box' and `white-box' methods. 

%Summarize Key Findings
Our findings highlight the hierarchy of challenges that afflict \gai, stemming from the foundational limitations of graph processing and impeding the fair evaluation of systems across the breadth of our \gai taxonomy.
We show how a problem-centric view of benchmarking is more flexible than a kernel-centric view and that it supports static and dynamic approaches in a single framework while encouraging innovative problem-solving. 
Most importantly, we show how the `grey-box' approach can use hypotheses to map input characteristics to expected outputs, giving benchmark designers the tools they need to detect the existence of bottlenecks in a \ac{SUT} without invasive system-level access.

%Reflect on significance
We have laid the groundwork for a new approach to \gai problem-solving by advocating for a more holistic evaluation framework that recognizes a \gai user may need to balance efficiency, effectiveness, and cost when deploying systems to complete \gai tasks.
We show how our approach would directly support an engineer designing a system architecture for solving \gai problems, providing a repeatable and extensible process to add new \gai problems as they become relevant to an organization's use case.

%Discuss Contributions
This thesis contributes literature reviews, methodological advancements, technical artifacts, and experimental findings to advance the field of \gai benchmarking. 
First, it synthesizes prior work by conducting four systematic literature reviews to develop the \gai taxonomy, the \ac{IandS} use-case survey, the \gai benchmarking analysis, and the \ac{CD} bottleneck analysis. 
Second, it contributes the \gai benchmarking framework and the methodology for \gai benchmark problem development, which is a novel fusion of traditional benchmarking, the empirical analysis of algorithms, and constraint theory into a `grey-box' approach.
Third, our technical contributions include a data generator and the benchmark observation apparatus. 
The data generator creates directed, weighted, attributed mesh graphs with ground truth communities at multiple resolution levels using real-world map data drawn from the \ac{OSM} database to fill an availability gap in real-world mesh graphs for evaluating \ac{CD}.
The observation apparatus is an \ac{API} between a \ac{SUT} and a \ac{BS} that implements a `polling period' to sample answers over time and compare static to dynamic techniques in the context of trading efficiency for effectiveness.
Finally, we contribute the specification for the \gai population segmentation benchmark and show how it can guide architectural decisions when designing and building \gai systems.

%Limitations and future work; Call to action. 
Looking ahead, further refinement of the benchmark framework, expansion to include additional \gai problems, and exploration of alternative evaluation metrics represent promising directions for future inquiry.
In summary, this thesis offers valuable insights into the challenges and opportunities of benchmarking \gai systems that will catalyze continued progress in this rapidly evolving field by promoting transparency, reproducibility, and problem-centric evaluation.
